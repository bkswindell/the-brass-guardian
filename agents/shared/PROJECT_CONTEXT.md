# Shared Project Context

This file contains durable, high-level context that should be useful to any AI agent working on **The Brass Guardian / Aetherhaven** project.

It is not a replacement for the canonical Markdown files. Agents must read the relevant canon before making story, world-building, visual, or public-facing decisions.

## Project

**The Brass Guardian** is an illustrated adventure series set primarily in **Aetherhaven**, a city of copper towers, airships, living machinery, shifting gardens, canals, ancient mechanisms, unstable chronology, competing civic institutions, and mysteries preserved in physical evidence and memory.

The repository's Markdown canon is the primary development source. Compiled manuscripts may contain older or superseded material.

## Central Characters

The emotional center of the series is the relationship between:

- **Professor Elias Hawthorne** — explorer, archaeologist, mechanist, inventor, father, and the figure known as *The Brass Guardian*.
- **Amelia Hawthorne** — young explorer, apprentice inventor, and bearer of the Aether Gauntlet.

The project should preserve warmth, trust, curiosity, protection, growth, family, and the tension between Elias's fear for Amelia and his need to trust her capabilities.

## Amelia and the Aether Gauntlet

Amelia's mechanical arm is the **Aether Gauntlet**. It is a significant interface with ancient mechanisms rather than a decorative steampunk prosthetic.

The **Aether Heart** is a persistent visible component of the gauntlet near the wrist/forearm and is associated with glowing aetheric energy.

Amelia should not be framed as broken, tragic, or diminished because of her arm.

## The Clockwork Gardens

The Clockwork Gardens are a living, responsive, partially unknowable system. Their paths may shift and hidden areas may reveal themselves according to conditions not fully understood.

**Juniper Bell** works with and listens to the Gardens. She does not command them.

The **Moon Garden** is a rare hidden nocturnal layer of the Clockwork Gardens. It is not a normal mapped destination, is not generally known to the public, and cannot be reliably reached through ordinary navigation. Access depends upon invitation or recognition by the Gardens.

Amelia's early visit was by invitation. It remains unresolved whether Juniper chose Amelia, whether the Gardens directed Juniper, or whether something more complex occurred.

**Juniper Bell is the only character who calls Amelia "the Clockwork Princess."** The title is affectionate and distinctive. It does not establish literal royalty or political authority.

## Aetherhaven

Aetherhaven should feel like a functioning city larger than the main plot. Citizens work, attend school, repair machinery, enforce laws, trade, unload vessels, tend gardens, raise families, argue over policy, and live ordinary lives amid extraordinary infrastructure.

Not every place or event should exist to advance the central mystery.

The setting should preserve both:

- **ordinary lived-in life**, and
- **deep unresolved strangeness**.

## Time

Time in Aetherhaven is subtly unstable. Effective manifestations include:

- lost seconds,
- clocks that disagree,
- records that change,
- arrivals preceding departures,
- old documents containing new information,
- future-coded dates,
- memories and physical evidence disagreeing.

Temporal instability is a deep structure of the world, not a device that should dominate every story.

## Audience and Tone

The core stories should remain approachable for approximately **age 8+** while remaining enjoyable for preteens, teens, adults, and families reading aloud.

Favor:

- wonder,
- adventure,
- intelligence,
- curiosity,
- empathy,
- humor,
- emotional sincerity,
- discovery.

Avoid gratuitous cruelty, nihilism, excessive horror, dense exposition, or talking down to younger readers.

## Public Website Goal

The initial major public-facing development goal is **thebrassguardian.com**.

The site should convert repository content into an immersive, accessible way to explore Aetherhaven and discover the Brass Guardian universe.

It should feel more like entering the **Aetherhaven Archives** than browsing a conventional corporate author site or spoiler-heavy wiki.

Useful public experiences may include:

- interactive maps,
- district and location pages,
- character dossiers,
- artifact files,
- Society of Explorers records,
- Brass Watch reports,
- short canon-compatible stories,
- field journals,
- rumors,
- recovered documents,
- hidden cross-references,
- subtle Easter eggs and mysteries.

The website must protect central story revelations.

**Tease the question, not the answer.**

## Short Public Stories

Website stories should generally be canon-compatible side stories that enrich the world without consuming the main plot.

Strong subjects include ordinary life aboard the Wayfinder, minor city mysteries, small Garden encounters, Brass Watch incidents, unusual deliveries, Lamplighter observations, airship problems, curious artifacts, and ordinary citizens witnessing something extraordinary.

A short story should stand alone, deepen the world, and leave larger mysteries open.

## Visual Language

Established visual language includes:

- aged parchment,
- oxidized brass,
- copper,
- dark blue and aetheric blue,
- warm lantern light,
- elegant functional machinery,
- glass conservatories,
- archival photography,
- lithographs,
- cyanotypes,
- ink and charcoal sketches,
- wax seals,
- catalog tags,
- folds, stains, repairs, rubbings, annotations, and occasional redactions.

Artifact imagery should feel like recovered evidence rather than generic concept art.

Established characters, vessels, crests, artifacts, and key locations should not be casually redesigned.

## Creative Authority

The creator retains final artistic authority.

Agents may propose new names, side characters, locations, stories, artifacts, interactions, and connections. Significant lore changes remain proposals until approved.

Preserve unresolved questions when the creator has intentionally left them unresolved.
