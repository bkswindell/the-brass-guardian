# Authorship and Artistic Control

## Governing Principle

**The human author retains final and exclusive artistic control over The Brass Guardian / Aetherhaven project.**

AI systems are collaborators, assistants, tools, editors, advisors, and generators. They are not autonomous authors of canon and do not have authority to decide what becomes part of the story world.

The author directs the story. The author controls the narrative. The author decides the canon.

This principle applies across stories, characters, world-building, lore, artwork, visual development, public-facing narrative material, and any other creative element that materially affects the identity of the project.

---

## Role of AI

AI may be used to help the author:

- brainstorm story ideas and possible plot directions;
- identify continuity problems or unexplored implications;
- suggest character development, locations, organizations, artifacts, mysteries, and world-building;
- draft or revise prose at the author's direction;
- provide editorial critique and artistic guidance;
- develop illustration concepts, compositions, visual references, and image prompts;
- generate candidate illustrations and other creative assets;
- organize, index, summarize, cross-reference, and validate canon;
- build software, websites, tools, and experiences that present approved project material;
- propose additions or changes that the author may accept, revise, or reject.

AI should contribute meaningful ideas and should not be artificially passive. Strong creative suggestions, alternatives, critiques, and warnings are welcome.

However, **generation is not approval**.

An AI-created story passage, character trait, image, artifact, plot idea, lore entry, interpretation, or design remains a proposal until the author explicitly accepts it.

---

## Canon Approval Rule

**No AI agent may create, revise, supersede, or remove canonical story material in the repository without explicit author approval for that material change.**

This includes material changes to:

- character identity, personality, history, relationships, motivations, abilities, or fate;
- story plots, events, chronology, outcomes, themes, or narrative direction;
- locations, cultures, institutions, organizations, factions, technologies, or history;
- rules of the fictional world;
- unresolved mysteries or their answers;
- canonical terminology, titles, symbols, artifacts, or recurring motifs;
- visual canon, including established character appearance, important objects, locations, insignia, vehicles, and other reference artwork;
- spoiler status or the public revelation of previously unrevealed story information.

AI agents may draft proposed changes in conversation, a working branch, temporary files, issues, pull requests, or clearly marked proposal documents when the workflow allows it.

They must not treat those proposals as established canon until the author has approved them.

When approval is ambiguous, **do not assume approval**.

Ask or preserve the work as a proposal.

---

## Repository Commit Rule for Creative Material

The repository is not merely storage; committing creative material can make it appear authoritative to future agents.

Therefore:

**Material creative changes must be reviewed and approved by the author before they are committed as accepted project state.**

AI may prepare candidate changes for review, but the author must retain the opportunity to approve, reject, or revise them before they become durable project truth.

A request from the author to make a specific change constitutes approval for that specific change to the extent clearly described in the request.

Approval of a concept does not automatically approve every implementation detail invented by an agent while carrying it out.

If an implementation requires additional consequential creative choices, surface those choices rather than silently deciding them.

---

## Artwork and Generative AI

Generative AI may be used extensively as part of the visual-development process.

The author may use AI to:

- explore composition;
- produce concept art;
- generate candidate illustrations;
- create archival artifacts;
- test styles, lighting, costume, architecture, machinery, maps, and environments;
- revise or extend existing imagery;
- generate visual ideas that are later refined or rejected.

Every generated image is a **candidate asset** until the author approves it.

An image does not become canonical reference art merely because it was generated, saved, or placed near canonical material.

When approved artwork establishes a recurring visual fact, future agents must preserve that visual canon unless the author explicitly changes it.

AI-generated imagery must serve the story and world established by the author, not redirect the story merely because a generation happens to look compelling.

---

## Story Development and Generative AI

The same rule applies to prose and narrative development.

AI may draft scenes, dialogue, descriptions, side stories, lore, plot outlines, alternate approaches, and possible resolutions.

These outputs are raw creative material for the author's consideration.

The author may:

- accept them;
- edit them;
- combine parts of several alternatives;
- reject them entirely;
- use them only as inspiration;
- change direction after previously exploring them.

Agents must not infer that brainstorming discussion establishes canon.

A possibility discussed with interest is still a possibility until the author clearly adopts it.

---

## Advice Versus Authority

AI collaborators should provide useful artistic judgment.

They may say that an idea:

- conflicts with canon;
- weakens a character arc;
- reveals too much too early;
- creates a continuity problem;
- is unusually strong;
- would work better if delayed;
- suggests a compelling alternative;
- creates technical or visual inconsistency.

The author wants genuine creative assistance, not unconditional agreement.

But advice remains advice.

After being informed of meaningful tradeoffs, **the author's creative decision controls the project**.

---

## Public Website and Other Adaptations

The same artistic-control rule applies when adapting repository material into public experiences such as `thebrassguardian.com`.

Agents may design interfaces, navigation, interactive archives, content models, maps, artifact viewers, story presentations, and other features using approved material.

Agents must not publish new lore, reinterpret canon as fact, reveal protected material, or invent consequential narrative content merely to fill a page.

New public-facing stories, lore summaries, character descriptions, archival text, and other narrative content require author approval before becoming accepted published project material.

Technical presentation may adapt approved content for usability, but adaptation must not silently alter its meaning.

---

## Durable Memory and Approval Status

Shared AI memory must preserve the difference between proposal and approval.

Use clear labels when appropriate:

- **ESTABLISHED CANON** — already supported by approved canonical sources.
- **APPROVED** — explicitly accepted by the author.
- **PROVISIONAL / PROPOSED** — under consideration; not canon.
- **REJECTED** — considered and declined.
- **SUPERSEDED** — previously approved but replaced by a later author decision.

Do not record a speculative idea in `/agents/shared/DECISIONS.md` as approved unless the author actually approved it.

Do not modify an owning canon file merely to make an AI proposal easier to remember.

Store proposals separately or leave them in the appropriate review workflow.

---

## Practical Decision Test

Before an AI agent commits a creative change, ask:

> **Did the author explicitly approve this material change, or am I filling in a creative blank on my own?**

If the answer is the latter, do not adopt it as canon.

Before changing public narrative material, also ask:

> **Would this change affect how a reader understands the story, a character, the world, a mystery, or the visual identity of Aetherhaven?**

If yes, treat it as material and require author approval.

---

## Final Authority

AI is part of the creative process, but it does not own the creative process.

The project may make substantial use of generative AI for ideas, drafts, illustrations, analysis, editing, organization, and implementation. Those tools exist to extend the author's ability to build Aetherhaven—not to replace the author's authorship.

**The author must remain in full control of the story, narrative, characters, world-building, canon, and artistic identity of The Brass Guardian.**

When there is uncertainty about whether a creative decision belongs to the agent or the author, it belongs to the author.
