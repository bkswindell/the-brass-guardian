# The High Council of Aetherhaven

## Canonical Summary

The High Council of Aetherhaven is the city's central governing body, responsible for law, trade, infrastructure, taxation, public safety, and relations among Aetherhaven's districts, guilds, and chartered institutions.

The Council's twelve active seats are divided between:

- one delegate from each of [the Eight Founding Engineering Guilds](The_Eight_Founding_Engineering_Guilds.md),
- and four civic representatives speaking for Aetherhaven's historic district quarters.

The Chancellor presides over the Council, administers its decisions, and serves as the public voice of the city.

Council members frequently disagree over commerce, labor, exploration, policing, district rights, mechanical personhood, public spending, and the proper limits of ancient technology.

They are united by one principle:

> **The Heart Engine must continue operating.**

This belief is formalized as the **Doctrine of Continuance** and treated as the foundation upon which every other law depends.

Behind the Chancellor's dais stands a sealed thirteenth chair.

It is reserved for the **First Mechanist**, an office older than the modern Council and supposedly empowered to overrule the Chancellor, command the founding guilds, and assume authority during a threat to Aetherhaven's continued existence.

No living citizen has seen the chair occupied.

Every formal session begins with the declaration:

> **The Twelve are called. The Thirteenth remains sealed.**

## Civic Role

The High Council governs matters that affect the city as a whole.

Its responsibilities include:

- civic law,
- taxation and public expenditure,
- trade regulation,
- district charters,
- labor and commercial disputes,
- canals and public transit,
- aerial commerce,
- infrastructure,
- public safety,
- emergency authority,
- civic calendars,
- official maps,
- and relations with outside settlements.

The Council does not directly operate every civic system.

It depends upon:

- [the Brass Watch](The_Brass_Watch.md) for enforcement and emergency response,
- [the Mechanists' Guild](The_Mechanists_Guild.md) for certification,
- the founding guilds for infrastructure,
- [the Aerial Mariners' Union](The_Aerial_Mariners_Union.md) for air traffic,
- the Conservancy for the Clockwork Gardens,
- district administrations for local services,
- and countless workers who can make Council policy either real or impossible.

The High Council is powerful.

It is not all-powerful.

## The Twelve Active Seats

The Council's structure reflects early Aetherhaven rather than the modern city.

Eight seats belong to the founding engineering guilds.

Four represent the historic civic quarters from which later districts developed.

This arrangement gives engineering institutions enormous political influence and leaves several modern districts feeling poorly represented.

### The Eight Guild Seats

Each of [the Eight Founding Engineering Guilds](The_Eight_Founding_Engineering_Guilds.md) appoints one **Council Delegate**:

1. The Framewright Seat
2. The Enginewright Seat
3. The Aetherwright Seat
4. The Canalwright Seat
5. The Skywright Seat
6. The Clockwright Seat
7. The Artificer Seat
8. The Verdant Seat

A Council Delegate is not automatically the guildmaster.

The distinction is intentional:

- the guildmaster leads the professional order and sits in the Conclave of Eight,
- the Council Delegate represents the guild in civic government.

Some guilds prohibit one person from holding both positions.

Others allow it during emergencies.

The eight Guild Seats ensure that no major civic law can ignore engineering reality.

They also make the Council deeply resistant to policies that threaten existing infrastructure or the Heart Engine.

### The Four Civic Seats

The four Civic Seats represent historic quarters whose boundaries predate many modern districts.

#### The Crown Seat

Represents the central civic and commercial quarter, including much of:

- the Government District,
- Merchant District,
- Grand Atrium,
- and surrounding central institutions.

The Crown Seat often focuses on:

- administration,
- finance,
- taxation,
- law,
- and public confidence.

#### The Forge Seat

Represents the city's heavy-working quarter, including:

- the Industrial District,
- Workers' Dormitories,
- Great Workshops,
- Engine Complex,
- and areas associated with the Gearbreaker Mines.

The Forge Seat is expected to represent both industrial owners and laboring residents.

In practice, those interests frequently conflict.

The High Council also claims [the Cauldron](../locations/The_Cauldron.md) falls within the Forge Seat's jurisdiction.

[The Cauldron](../locations/The_Cauldron.md) rejects that claim.

#### The Canal Seat

Represents districts shaped by water, trade, and older streets, including:

- Old City,
- Canal District,
- Southern Docks,
- and much of [the Entertainment District](../locations/The_Entertainment_District.md).

The Canal Seat often deals with:

- local trade,
- water control,
- old property rights,
- dock access,
- and neighborhoods built upon infrastructure older than current records.

#### The Spire Seat

Represents the elevated, exploratory, and inventive quarter, including:

- Inventors' District,
- Academy of Invention,
- [Aerial Docks](../locations/The_Aerial_Docks.md),
- Clockwork Gardens,
- Observatory,
- and nearby upper-city institutions.

The Spire Seat is influenced by:

- inventors,
- explorers,
- scholars,
- airship interests,
- and garden stewardship.

These groups rarely agree with one another.

## Representation Problems

Aetherhaven has outgrown the political structure that governs it.

The Four Civic Seats must speak for districts with very different needs, while the eight founding guilds retain individual representation.

This creates recurring criticism:

- workers share one seat with industrial leadership,
- [the Cauldron](../locations/The_Cauldron.md) has no recognized independent voice,
- [the Entertainment District](../locations/The_Entertainment_District.md) is represented through older canal interests,
- [the Aerial Mariners' Union](The_Aerial_Mariners_Union.md) has no permanent seat despite controlling aerial trade,
- the Conservancy has stewardship authority but no vote,
- and modern civic movements must lobby guild delegates whose professions may benefit from the current system.

Supporters argue that the founding structure prevents sudden political decisions from destroying systems the city depends upon.

Critics answer that it makes Aetherhaven a technocracy wearing the appearance of district government.

Both descriptions contain truth.

## The Chancellor

The Chancellor presides from a raised dais rather than one of the Twelve Seats.

The Chancellor:

- sets the public agenda,
- recognizes speakers,
- administers enacted law,
- coordinates civic departments,
- represents Aetherhaven externally,
- issues lawful emergency directives,
- and breaks tied votes.

The Chancellor cannot ordinarily compel the founding guilds to certify unsafe work or force independent organizations to perform tasks beyond their charters.

The office is therefore strongest through:

- coalition,
- information,
- public legitimacy,
- appointment power,
- and control of the Council's agenda.

The current Chancellor is **[Octavia](../characters/Chancellor_Octavia_Vale.md) Vale**.

She is intelligent, disciplined, politically cautious, and sincerely committed to Aetherhaven's survival.

She knows [the Order of the Closed Eye](The_Order_of_the_Closed_Eye.md) exists.

She does not understand how thoroughly it has shaped the institution she leads.

## Selection of the Chancellor

The Chancellor is elected by the Twelve from among qualified civic candidates.

A successful candidate must obtain support from both:

- the civic representatives,
- and the guild delegation.

This requirement is intended to prevent either bloc from controlling the government alone.

The Chancellor serves for a fixed term but may remain through successive elections.

Removal requires a supermajority or a finding of incapacity under the founding charter.

The First Mechanist, if recognized and seated, may possess older authority capable of suspending the Chancellor without such a vote.

No living jurist knows how that power would function in practice.

## The High Chamber

The Council meets in the **High Chamber** within the Government District.

The chamber is circular, designed to place the Twelve Seats around a central floor of debate.

Its major features include:

### The Twelve Seats

Brass-backed chairs and working desks arranged in two shallow arcs:

- eight marked with founding guild emblems,
- four bearing the symbols of the historic civic quarters.

### The Chancellor's Dais

A raised but comparatively plain position facing the Twelve.

The Chancellor's authority is intentionally separated from the numbered seats.

### The Public Gallery

A tiered gallery where citizens, petitioners, reporters, guild observers, and invited delegations may witness open proceedings.

### The Petition Floor

A marked circle from which citizens and organizations may formally address the Council.

Standing there places the speaker beneath the view of all Twelve Seats—and directly before the sealed Thirteenth.

### The Continuance Dial

A large mechanical indicator showing the officially reported operating condition of the Heart Engine.

It has never publicly displayed complete failure.

Some Clockwrights believe the dial reports political confidence rather than actual engine condition.

### The Thirteenth Chair

The sealed seat of the First Mechanist.

## The Thirteenth Chair

The Thirteenth Chair stands behind and above the Chancellor's dais.

It is enclosed by twelve interlocking brass shutters.

Each shutter bears one of the recognized civic or guild seals.

At the center is the **[First Mechanist's Mark](../artifacts/004_The_First_Mechanists_Mark.md)**.

The patterns beneath the chair's public decoration resemble:

- hidden symbols beneath old [Mechanists' Guild](The_Mechanists_Guild.md) medallions,
- access geometry inside the Heart Engine,
- and architectural forms predating modern Aetherhaven.

The chair has no visible keyhole.

No current official knows how it opens.

The Founding Charter states that the First Mechanist may:

- assume presidency of the Council,
- issue binding instructions to the Eight Founding Guilds,
- open sealed civic mechanisms,
- suspend ordinary procedure during a Continuance crisis,
- and overrule the Chancellor where the survival of Aetherhaven is directly concerned.

The same charter does not clearly explain:

- how the First Mechanist is selected,
- who confirms the office,
- whether the title belongs to one person,
- or whether the chair itself determines who may occupy it.

No living citizen has seen the chair occupied.

[The Order of the Closed Eye](The_Order_of_the_Closed_Eye.md) has suppressed several historical inquiries into the office.

## The First Mechanist

Public education describes the First Mechanist as the master engineer who coordinated the founding guilds during Aetherhaven's earliest construction.

Older documents suggest something more.

The title may have represented:

- chief architect of the early city,
- interpreter of the Heart Engine,
- holder of authority over all eight disciplines,
- guardian of emergency access,
- or a person recognized directly by ancient systems.

The First Mechanist is not simply the head of [the Mechanists' Guild](The_Mechanists_Guild.md).

The modern Guild did not exist in its current form when the office was created.

The Conclave of Eight has no accepted power to appoint a First Mechanist.

Some guild traditionalists believe the chair will open only when Aetherhaven requires one.

Others believe the office was deliberately eliminated because no person should hold such authority.

## The Doctrine of Continuance

Every Chancellor and Council Delegate swears to uphold the Doctrine of Continuance:

> **Whatever else we dispute, the Heart must continue.**

The doctrine arose from the belief that failure of the Heart Engine would cause:

- loss of power,
- collapse of Golden Vein systems,
- failure of water and transport,
- destabilization of the Clockwork Gardens,
- and possibly the destruction of Aetherhaven itself.

The doctrine is rational.

It is also the source of some of the Council's greatest failures.

Because the Heart must continue:

- dangerous evidence may be suppressed,
- alternatives may be underfunded,
- labor may be compelled,
- districts may be sacrificed,
- ancient systems may remain active without understanding,
- and dissent may be treated as an existential threat.

[The Unwound](The_Unwound.md) argues that the doctrine transformed dependence into law.

The Council argues that idealism does not keep the city alive.

## Continuance Protocols

During a declared Continuance emergency, the Council may authorize extraordinary measures.

These include:

- requisition of materials,
- mandatory engineering service,
- district closures,
- restricted travel,
- temporary control of private infrastructure,
- suspension of ordinary trade rules,
- sealed investigations,
- and transfer of artifacts or evidence into protected custody.

A Continuance declaration requires broad Council support.

Once invoked, it concentrates enormous authority in:

- the Chancellor,
- selected Guild Delegates,
- [the Brass Watch](The_Brass_Watch.md),
- and technical committees.

[The Order of the Closed Eye](The_Order_of_the_Closed_Eye.md) has repeatedly used Continuance procedures to give Closure the appearance of lawful necessity.

## Council Committees

Much of the Council's real work occurs before public votes.

### Committee of Works and Continuance

Oversees:

- Heart Engine policy,
- Golden Veins,
- major construction,
- Engine Complex,
- emergency infrastructure,
- and technical continuity.

It is the Council's most powerful committee.

### Committee of Trade and Passage

Oversees:

- markets,
- canals,
- docks,
- aerial commerce,
- tariffs,
- and transport law.

It negotiates with [the Aerial Mariners' Union](The_Aerial_Mariners_Union.md) during Closed Sky disputes.

### Committee of Civic Order

Oversees:

- law,
- [Brass Watch](The_Brass_Watch.md) funding,
- emergency procedure,
- detention standards,
- and district security.

[Chief Inspector Thorne](../characters/Chief_Inspector_Beatrice_Thorne.md) frequently appears before it.

### Committee of Record and Measure

Oversees:

- official maps,
- calendars,
- archives,
- standards,
- civic surveys,
- and public chronometry.

[The Order of the Closed Eye](The_Order_of_the_Closed_Eye.md) considers this committee strategically essential.

### Committee of Charters and Petitions

Reviews:

- district claims,
- organization charters,
- sanctuary disputes,
- personhood petitions,
- and proposed changes to Council representation.

It is where reform often slows without visibly being rejected.

## Open Bell and Closed Bell

Council proceedings are divided into two forms.

### Open Bell

Public sessions witnessed from the gallery.

These include:

- debate,
- petitions,
- ordinary legislation,
- budget matters,
- and public testimony.

### Closed Bell

Sealed sessions justified by:

- public safety,
- ancient mechanisms,
- trade negotiations,
- Continuance matters,
- or classified investigations.

Records from Closed Bell sessions may be delayed, redacted, or withheld.

The Order frequently operates through the boundary between legitimate secrecy and deliberate erasure.

## Political Blocs

The Council does not divide cleanly into permanent parties.

Temporary blocs form around issues.

### The Continuance Bloc

Prioritizes uninterrupted operation of the Heart Engine and existing civic systems.

Usually supported by:

- Aetherwright,
- Enginewright,
- and infrastructure interests.

### The Civic Reform Bloc

Seeks:

- broader district representation,
- labor protections,
- transparency,
- and limits on emergency authority.

Its membership changes by issue.

### The Expansion Bloc

Supports:

- exploration,
- aerial trade,
- new construction,
- and greater access to ancient technologies.

### The Caution Bloc

Favors:

- containment,
- slower experimentation,
- restricted excavation,
- and preservation of existing stability.

[The Order of the Closed Eye](The_Order_of_the_Closed_Eye.md) influences parts of the Caution Bloc without controlling every cautious member.

### The Independence Bloc

Resists control by:

- guilds,
- the Chancellor,
- distant trade interests,
- or central government.

Its members may agree on little else.

## The Order of the Closed Eye

The Order exercises more control over the High Council than the Council realizes.

At any given time, multiple seats may be:

- **Open Seats**, knowingly occupied by Order members,
- **Bound Seats**, controlled through leverage, oath, altered records, or Veiling,
- **Guided Seats**, influenced through aides, briefing materials, committee staff, or curated evidence,
- or **Unbound Seats**, genuinely outside direct control.

The exact number remains unknown.

The Order's greatest power is not commanding votes.

It determines:

- what evidence reaches the chamber,
- which candidates appear acceptable,
- which records are available,
- which crises receive Continuance classification,
- and which choices seem possible.

[Chancellor Octavia Vale](../characters/Chancellor_Octavia_Vale.md) believes the Order is a constrained emergency society.

She does not know how much of the Council's institutional memory belongs to it.

For complete hidden canon, see [The Order of the Closed Eye](The_Order_of_the_Closed_Eye.md).

## Relationship with the Mechanists' Guild

The High Council's eight Guild Seats are held by delegates from the founding orders.

[The Mechanists' Guild](The_Mechanists_Guild.md) provides:

- shared standards,
- certification,
- professional discipline,
- and the framework through which those delegates coordinate.

The Guild does not own the seats.

Each founding order retains the right to appoint its own delegate.

The Conclave of Eight and High Council may disagree.

The Council decides what the law requires.

The Conclave decides whether qualified professionals will certify or perform the work.

The sealed Thirteenth Chair is not controlled by either body.

For complete guild canon, see:

- [The Mechanists' Guild](The_Mechanists_Guild.md)
- [The Eight Founding Engineering Guilds](The_Eight_Founding_Engineering_Guilds.md)

## Relationship with the Brass Watch

[The Brass Watch](The_Brass_Watch.md) enforces Council law and responds to civic emergencies.

It is funded and overseen by the Council but retains operational independence under its charter.

The Council may:

- create laws,
- authorize investigations,
- approve budgets,
- establish emergency powers,
- and demand testimony.

It should not dictate:

- an individual arrest,
- a false finding,
- or the alteration of evidence.

In practice, officials sometimes try.

[Chief Inspector Thorne](../characters/Chief_Inspector_Beatrice_Thorne.md)'s refusal to turn procedure into political theater creates recurring conflict with Council committees.

The Order's sealed directives complicate the relationship by appearing to carry authority beyond ordinary Council review.

## Relationship with the Aerial Mariners' Union

[The Aerial Mariners' Union](The_Aerial_Mariners_Union.md) has no permanent Council seat.

This omission is a recurring political grievance.

The Skywright Seat represents vessel engineering.

It does not represent the captains, navigators, engineers, and dockworkers who operate the routes.

The Union therefore relies upon:

- public testimony,
- negotiation,
- labor pressure,
- and the Closed Sky.

A Closed Sky declaration can force the Council to negotiate more effectively than any formal petition.

The Council depends on the Union and resents that dependence.

## Relationship with the Conservancy of Living Mechanisms

The Conservancy has no voting seat.

It operates under a special stewardship charter granting authority over:

- the Clockwork Gardens,
- living mechanisms under its care,
- Garden closure,
- and Living Stay protections.

The Spire Seat claims to represent the Gardens politically.

The Conservancy does not accept that representation as ownership.

It rarely seeks ordinary power.

When threatened systems are involved, it may refuse the Council completely.

## Relationship with the Academy of Invention

The Academy has no independent seat.

Its interests usually reach the Council through:

- the Spire Seat,
- the Artificer Seat,
- the Aetherwright Seat,
- invited experts,
- and committee testimony.

The Academy resents Guild control over certification.

The Council fears innovation it cannot regulate and covets innovation that increases trade or Continuance.

The Order maintains deep influence within the Academy-Council relationship.

## Relationship with the Society of Explorers

The Society of Explorers advises the Council on:

- distant routes,
- recovered artifacts,
- restricted regions,
- and expedition policy.

It has no permanent seat.

The Spire and Skywright delegates usually sponsor its petitions.

The Council funds discoveries and then determines:

- what may be published,
- what enters the Archives,
- and what requires restricted custody.

This makes the Society both partner and victim of Council secrecy.

## Relationship with the Cauldron

The High Council claims [the Cauldron](../locations/The_Cauldron.md) is legally part of Aetherhaven and nominally represented through the Forge Seat.

[The Cauldron](../locations/The_Cauldron.md) rejects both claims.

The Cinder Regent and Furnace Court argue that:

- the Council does not protect the district,
- the Forge Seat does not speak for it,
- and laws imposed without meaningful access or representation have no legitimacy.

The Ash Compact exists because neither side can fully impose its preferred reality.

The Council condemns [the Cauldron](../locations/The_Cauldron.md) publicly while depending on its:

- salvage,
- repair,
- waste processing,
- forbidden expertise,
- and gray trade.

## Relationship with the Unwound

The broader [Unwound](The_Unwound.md) sees the Council as structurally dependent upon the systems it is supposed to govern.

Its main criticisms include:

- eight guild seats outweighing four civic seats,
- the Doctrine of Continuance suppressing alternatives,
- emergency powers becoming permanent habits,
- and citizens being denied knowledge required for meaningful consent.

Moderate [Unwound](The_Unwound.md) groups petition for:

- manual backups,
- transparent records,
- district representation,
- and limits on Heart Engine dependence.

[The Severed Coil](The_Severed_Coil.md) treats the Council as proof the city cannot reform peacefully.

The Council's treatment of moderate reformers can either isolate or strengthen the extremists.

## Relationship with the Underclock

The Council publicly treats [the Underclock](The_Underclock.md) as an illicit smuggling and sanctuary network.

Individual councillors, staff members, and political families privately use it for:

- secret messages,
- protected meetings,
- forbidden records,
- and emergency escape.

[The Underclock](The_Underclock.md) exists partly because citizens do not trust Council systems to protect them fairly.

That makes it both a criminal problem and evidence of civic failure.

## Relationship with the Ninth Guild

The Council publicly denies that [the Ninth Guild](The_Ninth_Guild.md) exists as a coherent institution.

[Black Lacuna](The_Ninth_Guild.md) devices are classified as:

- isolated criminal inventions,
- contraband,
- failed research,
- or unrelated incidents.

Privately, some councillors have:

- purchased Guild services,
- hidden research through Guild channels,
- sought memory revision,
- or traded confiscated artifacts for information.

The Council does not control [the Ninth Guild](The_Ninth_Guild.md).

Its hypocrisy helps the Guild survive.

## Relationship with the Lamplighters' Fellowship

The Council funds portions of the city's public lighting system and treats [the Fellowship](The_Lamplighters_Fellowship.md) as a dependable civic service.

Lamplighter testimony occasionally appears before Council committees.

Their Night Books can contradict:

- official maps,
- Watch reports,
- and Closed Bell records.

Council officials may order entries sealed.

[The Lamplighters](The_Lamplighters_Fellowship.md) generally return to work and continue remembering what they saw.

## Relationship with Elias Hawthorne

Elias is frequently summoned as:

- expert witness,
- investigator,
- engineer,
- explorer,
- or reluctant advisor.

Some councillors respect his judgment.

Others consider him dangerously independent.

He distrusts the Council's tendency to seek:

- certainty without evidence,
- expertise without autonomy,
- and solutions without accepting responsibility.

Elias may cooperate when lives are at risk.

He resists any attempt to classify Amelia, the Aether Heart, or the Wayfinder as civic property.

## Relationship with Amelia Hawthorne

The Council cannot agree what Amelia represents.

She may be viewed as:

- a child requiring protection,
- the Bearer of the Living Key,
- a witness,
- a public-safety risk,
- a link to ancient machinery,
- or an individual whose rights are threatened by every institution seeking access.

[Octavia](../characters/Chancellor_Octavia_Vale.md) Vale insists Amelia must be addressed as a person rather than a device.

Not every councillor agrees privately.

The Thirteenth Chair may eventually respond to Amelia's presence.

That possibility remains a staged revelation and must not establish that Amelia is the First Mechanist.

## Temporal Significance

The Council controls official:

- calendars,
- maps,
- public minutes,
- legal dates,
- and civic records.

When time adjusts, the government determines which version becomes administratively real.

This makes it central to Aetherhaven's temporal instability.

Council records may contain:

- votes whose participants deny attending,
- laws dated before their proposal,
- district boundaries changed without amendment,
- and Closed Bell minutes referencing evidence no longer in the Archives.

The Committee of Record and Measure treats most contradictions as clerical error.

[The Order of the Closed Eye](The_Order_of_the_Closed_Eye.md) knows better.

The sealed Thirteenth Chair may be one of the city's strongest temporal anchors.

### The Twelve-and-One Seal

Twelve visible brass points surrounding one closed central mark.

The public seal usually emphasizes the Twelve.

Older versions make the central absence more prominent.

### The Council Bell

Rung to open formal proceedings.

A different, lower tone marks Closed Bell sessions.

### The Continuance Oath

Spoken by every Chancellor and Council Delegate.

### The Empty Declaration

Every formal session begins:

> **The Twelve are called. The Thirteenth remains sealed.**

### The First Mechanist's Mark

Displayed upon the sealed chair and connected to hidden guild and Heart Engine geometry.

## Visual Continuity

The High Council should feel:

- grand but functional,
- old beneath later decoration,
- ceremonial without becoming royal,
- and visibly shaped by engineering authority.

Recurring visual elements include:

- brass-backed seats,
- district and guild seals,
- mechanical voting indicators,
- public galleries,
- layered documents,
- civic clocks,
- the Continuance Dial,
- and the sealed Thirteenth Chair looming behind the Chancellor.

The Thirteenth Chair should appear ancient, restrained, and more technologically significant than ornamental.

- [Octavia](../characters/Chancellor_Octavia_Vale.md) Vale is Chancellor.
- Council members disagree frequently.
- All uphold the Doctrine of Continuance.
- The sealed Thirteenth Chair belongs to the First Mechanist.
- No living citizen has seen it occupied.
- The First Mechanist's authority supposedly exceeds the Chancellor's.

- The eight Guild Seats structurally outweigh the four Civic Seats.
- Several modern districts lack meaningful direct representation.

- The Chancellor presides separately from the Twelve.
- The current Chancellor is [Octavia](../characters/Chancellor_Octavia_Vale.md) Vale.
- The Doctrine of Continuance unites otherwise divided members.
- The Heart Engine's continued operation must be treated as both a legitimate survival concern and a source of institutional blindness.
- The Thirteenth Chair is reserved for the First Mechanist.
- No living citizen has seen the chair occupied.
- The First Mechanist's authority supposedly exceeds that of the Chancellor.
- The First Mechanist is not simply the modern head of [the Mechanists' Guild](The_Mechanists_Guild.md).
- [The Order of the Closed Eye](The_Order_of_the_Closed_Eye.md) influences multiple seats and the information reaching the Council, but does not control every member or outcome.
- [The Aerial Mariners' Union](The_Aerial_Mariners_Union.md), Conservancy, Academy, Society of Explorers, and [Brass Watch](The_Brass_Watch.md) do not possess ordinary voting seats.
- [The Cauldron](../locations/The_Cauldron.md) rejects representation through the Forge Seat.
- Amelia must never become civic property.
- A response from the Thirteenth Chair must not automatically identify Amelia as the First Mechanist.
- The Council should be capable of courage, compromise, cowardice, and genuine reform.
- The sealed chair and Continuance structure must remain central long-range mysteries.
