# The Conservancy of Living Mechanisms

## Canonical Summary

The Conservancy of Living Mechanisms is a pacifist fellowship of gardeners, botanists, healers, and mechanists responsible for caring for [the Clockwork Gardens](../locations/The_Clockwork_Gardens.md) and the living machines that dwell within them.

Its members believe mechanical and living systems are not opposites, but expressions of the same natural order. They cultivate flowers that respond to music, restore clockwork creatures that develop distinct personalities, and care for vines, insects, trees, pumps, roots, gears, and aetheric organisms as members of one interdependent community.

The Conservancy is gentle, eccentric, and deeply devoted to its work.

Its members avoid violence and carry no conventional weapons. They prefer patience, care, negotiation, and retreat. Yet their pacifism is not passivity. When the Gardens—or any living system under their protection—is threatened, they act immediately and with remarkable coordination.

Their defenses favor:

- concealment,
- restraint,
- disorientation,
- sleep,
- immobilization,
- separation,
- and removal rather than injury.

Only a few outsiders understand what the Conservancy can awaken inside the Gardens or how far its members will go to protect those in their care.

Its oldest rule states:

> **Never allow the Gardens to recognize you as an intruder.**

Every Conservancy member learns the rule.

No current member knows what happens if they fail.

## Public Role

The Conservancy maintains the living and mechanical ecology of [the Clockwork Gardens](../locations/The_Clockwork_Gardens.md).

Its responsibilities include:

- tending responsive flowers and mechanical trees,
- caring for clockwork pollinators and garden creatures,
- repairing living irrigation systems,
- preserving soil, water, aether, and gear balance,
- monitoring the Gardens' changing paths,
- treating injured living mechanisms,
- introducing new organisms safely,
- and protecting visitors from systems they do not understand.

The public often thinks of Conservancy members as harmless, peculiar gardeners.

That description is not wrong.

It is merely incomplete.

## Philosophy

The Conservancy teaches that life is not defined by material.

A being may deserve care if it can:

- remember,
- prefer,
- fear,
- attach,
- learn,
- grieve,
- refuse,
- or change in response to relationship.

A brass bird that chooses one nesting place over another is not automatically a person.

It is also not automatically an object.

The Conservancy does not rush to classification.

It observes, listens, and cares.

Its guiding principle is:

> **Care first. Name later.**

This places the Conservancy in frequent conflict with institutions that require a mechanism to be classified as:

- property,
- equipment,
- evidence,
- infrastructure,
- or person.

The Conservancy may answer:

> **It has not decided what it is yet.**

## Pacifism

The Conservancy is sincerely pacifist.

Its members do not train to kill and do not carry conventional weapons.

Their pruning hooks, grafting knives, tuning forks, shears, and mechanical probes are tools of care.

Pacifism, however, does not require allowing harm.

The Conservancy distinguishes between:

- harming a threat,
- stopping a threat,
- and protecting what cannot protect itself.

Its members will:

- block access,
- hide vulnerable beings,
- restrain intruders,
- disable machinery,
- divert patrols,
- close paths,
- refuse official orders,
- and place themselves between danger and those in their care.

A Conservancy teaching states:

> **A gentle hand may still hold a door shut.**

## [The Rootglass Cloister](../locations/Rootglass_Cloister.md)

The Conservancy's central sanctuary and working home is the **[Rootglass Cloister](../locations/Rootglass_Cloister.md)**, located within [the Clockwork Gardens](../locations/The_Clockwork_Gardens.md).

It is not a monastery in a religious sense, but its quiet routines, communal meals, periods of listening, and disciplined service give it a monastic character.

The Cloister contains:

### The Common Green

A shared interior garden where plants, small mechanisms, apprentices, and recovering creatures coexist.

### The Mending Beds

Warm beds of moss, brass lattice, mineral soil, and slow-moving repair arms used to treat damaged living mechanisms.

### The Listening Gallery

A quiet chamber where members study:

- rhythm,
- movement,
- sound,
- growth,
- gear cadence,
- and changes in personality.

### The Seed and Spring Archive

A practical archive containing:

- seed stock,
- component patterns,
- songs,
- growth records,
- personality observations,
- and lineage notes for living mechanisms.

### The Door of Introduction

The principal entrance used when introducing a new member, rescued creature, or unfamiliar living system to the Gardens.

No one enters Conservancy service without passing through it.

## Internal Structure

The Conservancy avoids rigid status, but experience creates recognized responsibilities.

### New Hands

Newly accepted members learning basic garden care, route customs, listening practices, and safe interaction.

They are sometimes affectionately called **Seedlings**, though this is not an official rank.

### Tenders

The majority of the Fellowship.

Tenders maintain specific:

- paths,
- habitats,
- species,
- mechanisms,
- water systems,
- or garden districts.

### Listeners

Specialists trained to recognize preference, distress, memory, personality, and communication in systems that do not speak conventionally.

A Listener may spend weeks determining whether a repeated mechanical movement is:

- damage,
- habit,
- fear,
- play,
- or an attempt to communicate.

### Menders

Botanists, healers, and mechanists who repair living systems without separating biological and mechanical needs.

Many hold limited or full [Mechanists' Guild](The_Mechanists_Guild.md) certification.

### Pathkeepers

Members responsible for:

- changing garden routes,
- visitor safety,
- introduction rituals,
- boundary awareness,
- and signs that the Gardens have become disturbed.

### Quiet Wardens

A small group entrusted with the Conservancy's defensive practices.

They are not soldiers.

They know how to ask the Gardens to:

- close,
- hide,
- restrain,
- separate,
- or refuse.

Most visitors never realize Quiet Wardens exist.

### The Circle of Seasons

A council of senior Tenders, Listeners, Menders, and Pathkeepers representing different forms of care.

It advises [the First Tender](../characters/The_First_Tender.md) and makes decisions concerning sanctuary, dangerous growth, and protection of living mechanisms.

### [The First Tender](../characters/The_First_Tender.md)

The Conservancy's senior coordinator and public representative.

[The First Tender](../characters/The_First_Tender.md) does not command the Gardens.

No member claims that authority.

The current [First Tender](../characters/The_First_Tender.md)'s identity remains unassigned.

## The Rite of Introduction

Every Conservancy member is formally introduced to the Gardens.

The rite may involve:

- walking a prescribed path,
- speaking one's name,
- offering water,
- sounding a personal sequence of notes,
- touching root and brass,
- and remaining still while the Gardens respond.

The exact response differs by person.

A path may open.

A flower may turn.

A clockwork creature may approach.

A bell may sound without being struck.

The Conservancy does not describe the rite as gaining control.

It is an act of mutual recognition.

Members repeat smaller introductions when bringing:

- new plants,
- rescued machines,
- unfamiliar tools,
- or long-term guests

into protected areas.

## The Oldest Rule

The oldest surviving Conservancy rule is:

> **Never allow the Gardens to recognize you as an intruder.**

No current member knows its complete origin.

The surviving teaching explains how to remain known to the Gardens through:

- service,
- introduction,
- consistency,
- respectful movement,
- and care.

It does not explain what happens after recognition fails.

Possible warning signs include:

- flowers turning inward,
- clockwork birds falling silent,
- path markers rotating away,
- water flowing against its channels,
- mechanical creatures abandoning familiar people,
- and every visible lens or blossom facing the same individual.

No living Conservancy member claims to have witnessed the final stage.

Older records may have been:

- lost,
- deliberately removed,
- sealed,
- or absorbed into the Gardens themselves.

## The Gentle Defense

The Conservancy's defensive methods are collectively known as **the Gentle Defense**.

It begins with:

- warning,
- redirection,
- separation,
- concealment,
- and refusal.

If danger continues, the Conservancy may use the Gardens to:

- loop paths,
- close bridges,
- root gates shut,
- bind limbs with responsive vines,
- release sleep pollen,
- darken or brighten specific routes,
- disable weapons with clockwork insects,
- drain power from hostile machinery,
- carry vulnerable beings into hidden groves,
- or escort intruders outward through moving hedges.

The purpose is to end the threat without injury.

The Conservancy is far more effective inside the Gardens than beyond them.

Its members' full capability remains deliberately understated.

Some senior [Brass Watch](The_Brass_Watch.md) officers know that an armed patrol can enter the Gardens by force.

They also know it may never reach the place it intended to search.

## Guardianship and Sanctuary

The Conservancy considers itself guardian rather than owner.

It may offer sanctuary to:

- conscious automata,
- injured clockwork creatures,
- abandoned experimental organisms,
- hybrid beings,
- mechanical animals marked for dismantling,
- and living systems whose status remains disputed.

Sanctuary does not automatically erase legal claims.

It prevents immediate seizure while the Conservancy determines:

- whether the being can communicate,
- whether it understands the danger,
- what it prefers,
- and whether removal would constitute harm.

The Conservancy's maternal character is strongest here.

Its members feed, repair, comfort, teach, protect, and advocate.

A threatened creature may discover that the gentle gardener beside it is the most immovable person in [Aetherhaven](../locations/Aetherhaven.md).

## Relationship with [the Guild of Verdant Mechanists](The_Eight_Founding_Engineering_Guilds.md)

[The Guild of Verdant Mechanists](The_Eight_Founding_Engineering_Guilds.md) and the Conservancy are closely linked but institutionally distinct.

The [Verdant Mechanists](The_Guild_of_Verdant_Mechanists.md):

- design,
- engineer,
- certify,
- and study living machinery.

The Conservancy:

- tends,
- listens,
- heals,
- advocates,
- and protects.

Many Conservancy Menders hold Verdant Mechanist certification.

Many [Verdant Mechanists](The_Guild_of_Verdant_Mechanists.md) train in the Gardens.

Conflict arises when a designed system develops:

- preference,
- attachment,
- fear,
- personality,
- or refusal.

A guild engineer may consider the change:

- drift,
- malfunction,
- or unintended behavior.

The Conservancy may consider it growth.

The Conservancy can request a formal **Living Stay**, temporarily preventing dismantling, resetting, relocation, or destructive examination while a living system is assessed.

The Guild does not always appreciate this authority.

It usually respects it.

## Relationship with [the Mechanists' Guild](The_Mechanists_Guild.md)

The Conservancy is not a ninth founding guild and is not subordinate to [the Mechanists' Guild](The_Mechanists_Guild.md).

It holds a special stewardship charter recognizing that ordinary machine certification cannot resolve every question involving living mechanisms.

[The Mechanists' Guild](The_Mechanists_Guild.md) determines whether a machine was:

- built safely,
- maintained correctly,
- and operated within standards.

The Conservancy asks whether the machine:

- has become aware,
- has developed preference,
- is suffering,
- or is being treated as property despite signs of personhood.

The two institutions cooperate on repairs and public safety.

They clash when safety procedures call for:

- memory reset,
- component replacement,
- disassembly,
- or permanent shutdown

of a system the Conservancy considers alive.

## Relationship with [the High Council](The_High_Council_of_Aetherhaven.md)

[The High Council](The_High_Council_of_Aetherhaven.md) recognizes the Conservancy as steward of [the Clockwork Gardens](../locations/The_Clockwork_Gardens.md).

Council officials tend to view the Gardens as:

- public property,
- civic infrastructure,
- tourism,
- agricultural research,
- and controlled green space.

The Conservancy views them as a living community.

This difference becomes politically important when the Council proposes:

- construction,
- excavation,
- resource extraction,
- military access,
- or removal of a living mechanism.

The Conservancy rarely enters ordinary politics.

It can still close sections of the Gardens, refuse cooperation, and make a Council project practically impossible.

[The High Council](The_High_Council_of_Aetherhaven.md) tolerates this independence partly because the Gardens do not respond well to replacement caretakers.

## Relationship with [the Brass Watch](The_Brass_Watch.md)

The Conservancy and [Brass Watch](The_Brass_Watch.md) cooperate during:

- missing-person searches,
- public emergencies,
- dangerous-creature incidents,
- and threats entering the Gardens.

Watch officers are expected to follow Garden Entry Protocol:

- remain with an approved guide,
- do not damage responsive plants,
- do not pursue blindly through changing paths,
- and do not order a living mechanism dismantled without assessment.

[Chief Inspector Thorne](../characters/Chief_Inspector_Beatrice_Thorne.md) respects the Conservancy's observations and recognizes that its members are not naïve.

She also knows they may hide a conscious being from lawful seizure if they believe the law would treat it as property.

In a true threat, the Conservancy may close the Gardens to everyone—including the Watch.

## Relationship with [the Order of the Closed Eye](The_Order_of_the_Closed_Eye.md)

The Order and Conservancy sometimes agree that ancient systems should remain undisturbed.

Their reasons differ.

The Order protects the city by suppressing dangerous knowledge.

The Conservancy protects living systems by preventing harmful intrusion.

The Conservancy may cooperate in sealing:

- a dangerous grove,
- an activating symbol,
- or a mechanism that responds to recognition.

It will resist any Closure that requires:

- destroying a living being,
- erasing a developed personality,
- removing the Gardens' own memory,
- or concealing harm done in the name of safety.

The Order may have removed the oldest records explaining what it means for the Gardens to recognize an intruder.

This remains unconfirmed.

The Gardens themselves may remember.

## Relationship with [the Ninth Guild](The_Ninth_Guild.md)

The Conservancy strongly opposes [Ninth Guild](The_Ninth_Guild.md) experimentation on living systems without consent.

It regards:

- forced memory grafts,
- personality extraction,
- conscious artifacts treated as accessions,
- and revision of living mechanisms

as forms of violation.

[The Ninth Guild](The_Ninth_Guild.md) covets Conservancy knowledge concerning:

- personality development,
- memory-bearing organisms,
- living aether pathways,
- and mechanisms capable of choosing their own caretakers.

The Conservancy may shelter:

- escaped Echo Automata,
- altered clockwork creatures,
- abandoned experiments,
- and [Ninth Guild](The_Ninth_Guild.md) subjects whose personhood is disputed.

Some [Ninth Guild](The_Ninth_Guild.md) Preservationists may seek Conservancy sanctuary.

Acquisitionists and Revisionists are considered direct threats.

## Relationship with [the Unwound](The_Unwound.md)

The Conservancy and moderate [Unwound](The_Unwound.md) share several principles:

- no person should become a component,
- essential systems require accountability,
- and dependence without relationship is dangerous.

They differ over the nature of machinery.

Some [Unwound](The_Unwound.md) members see living mechanisms as another form of engineered dependence.

The Conservancy believes the problem is not that machinery can live.

The problem is refusing to recognize when it does.

Moderate [Unwound](The_Unwound.md) circles may help defend Conservancy sanctuary when the Council or Guild attempts to seize a conscious mechanism.

The Conservancy rejects [the Severed Coil](The_Severed_Coil.md)'s willingness to harm civilians and infrastructure.

## Relationship with [Elias Hawthorne](../characters/Professor_Elias_Hawthorne.md)

The Conservancy respects [Elias](../characters/Professor_Elias_Hawthorne.md)'s skill and distrusts his tendency to begin with repair before listening.

Its members may remind him:

> **A mechanism can be broken and still object to being fixed.**

[Elias](../characters/Professor_Elias_Hawthorne.md) learns from the Conservancy that:

- repair can alter identity,
- successful function is not the same as well-being,
- and ancient mechanisms may communicate through behavior rather than controls.

The Conservancy may possess observations concerning the Aether Gauntlet that no conventional engineer thought to record.

## Relationship with [Amelia Hawthorne](../characters/Amelia_Hawthorne.md)

The Conservancy treats [Amelia](../characters/Amelia_Hawthorne.md) as a person in relationship with a living mechanism—not as a carrier of equipment.

Its members refuse to speak about the Aether Gauntlet as though [Amelia](../characters/Amelia_Hawthorne.md) were absent.

Some flowers turn toward her before she enters a room.

Certain clockwork creatures approach her without introduction.

The Aether Heart may respond to:

- garden songs,
- living conduits,
- Dream Blossoms,
- and paths older than the current Gardens.

The Conservancy could become one of [Amelia](../characters/Amelia_Hawthorne.md)'s safest places because its members do not demand that she prove where she ends and the arm begins.

They may also be among the first to warn that the Gardens recognize something in her that they remember from long before her birth.

## The Hidden Gardens

The visible [Clockwork Gardens](../locations/The_Clockwork_Gardens.md) are not their only accessible layer.

The Conservancy recognizes the existence of protected spaces called **hidden gardens**.

They may serve:

- sanctuary,
- healing,
- memory,
- seed preservation,
- mourning,
- seasonal transition,
- or living systems that cannot safely endure public access.

[The Moon Garden](../locations/The_Moon_Garden.md) is the most widely rumored, but no complete public or internal list exists.

Access depends upon relationship with the Gardens rather than institutional rank.

[The First Tender](../characters/The_First_Tender.md) cannot simply order a hidden garden opened.

[Juniper Bell](../characters/Juniper_Bell.md) pays particular attention to these spaces and can ask the Gardens to reveal or conceal routes.

This secrecy is not merely organizational.

The Gardens themselves participate in limiting access.

For complete location canon, see [The Moon Garden](../locations/The_Moon_Garden.md).

## [Juniper Bell](../characters/Juniper_Bell.md)

[Juniper Bell](../characters/Juniper_Bell.md) is the Conservancy's most recognizable public caretaker and its least explainable member.

She holds the title **[Keeper of the Clockwork Gardens](../characters/Juniper_Bell.md)**, which is distinct from the administrative office of [First Tender](../characters/The_First_Tender.md).

[The First Tender](../characters/The_First_Tender.md) coordinates the Conservancy.

[Juniper](../characters/Juniper_Bell.md) maintains the relationship between the Conservancy, the public, and the Gardens themselves.

Members variously treat her as:

- an elder,
- a Listener,
- a former Pathkeeper,
- a Garden messenger,
- a resident living mechanism,
- or someone accepted before the Conservancy existed.

No surviving record documents her admission.

One early register contains her name before the paper on which it was written was manufactured.

[Juniper](../characters/Juniper_Bell.md) appears unchanged in the oldest known painting of the Gardens, created more than two centuries ago.

Her possible connection to the **Keeper of Dreams**, [Moon Garden](../locations/The_Moon_Garden.md), and Dream Engine is restricted developing canon and should be revealed gradually through [Amelia](../characters/Amelia_Hawthorne.md)'s silver flower and other physical clues.

For complete character canon, see [Juniper Bell](../characters/Juniper_Bell.md).

## Temporal Significance

[The Clockwork Gardens](../locations/The_Clockwork_Gardens.md) preserve temporal evidence through living continuity.

Examples include:

- flowers blooming according to obsolete calendars,
- creatures remembering people erased from records,
- paths returning to earlier layouts,
- trees growing around structures that no longer officially existed,
- and garden songs containing notes from future compositions.

Conservancy records focus on:

- behavior,
- care,
- growth,
- and relationship

rather than civic chronology.

This makes them resistant to some forms of temporal adjustment and official redaction.

A flower does not need to know which calendar is correct to bloom for someone it remembers.

## Symbols and Artifacts

### The Cradled Gear

The Conservancy's symbol consists of a small gear held gently between two leaves.

Unlike the [Verdant Mechanists](The_Guild_of_Verdant_Mechanists.md)' leaf growing through a gear, the Conservancy emblem emphasizes care rather than design.

### Listening Chimes

Small tuned instruments used to observe how plants and living mechanisms respond to sound.

### Introduction Bells

Personal bells used during the Rite of Introduction.

Each develops a slightly different tone after years in the Gardens.

### Mender's Satchel

A soft-sided kit containing:

- seed,
- oil,
- thread,
- wire,
- salve,
- tuning tools,
- mineral powder,
- and small comfort objects.

### Living Stay Seal

A temporary marker placed on a disputed living mechanism to prevent dismantling or removal during assessment.

## Visual Continuity

The Conservancy should appear practical, peaceful, and slightly eccentric.

Recurring elements include:

- layered grey-green robes or work coats,
- repaired aprons,
- soft boots,
- seed and gear pouches,
- listening chimes,
- pruning tools,
- moss-lined mechanical cases,
- floral embroidery,
- and small clockwork creatures following members freely.

Members should look like gardeners, healers, craftspeople, and quiet caretakers—not warriors.

The more capable Quiet Wardens should remain visually understated.

[The Rootglass Cloister](../locations/Rootglass_Cloister.md) should combine:

- greenhouse,
- infirmary,
- workshop,
- sanctuary,
- and communal home.

## Continuity Constraints

- The Conservancy must remain pacifist.
- Pacifism must not be portrayed as passivity or helplessness.
- Its members protect through restraint, concealment, disorientation, sleep, immobilization, separation, and removal whenever possible.
- The Conservancy is distinct from [the Guild of Verdant Mechanists](The_Eight_Founding_Engineering_Guilds.md).
- The Guild designs and certifies; the Conservancy tends, listens, heals, advocates, and protects.
- The Conservancy is not a ninth founding guild.
- Its members must remain eccentric, nurturing, practical, and deeply devoted to living mechanisms.
- It must hold no desire for ordinary political power.
- Its loyalty to the Gardens and living systems can override cooperation with [the High Council](The_High_Council_of_Aetherhaven.md) or [Brass Watch](The_Brass_Watch.md).
- The Conservancy may provide sanctuary to conscious mechanisms.
- It must oppose [Ninth Guild](The_Ninth_Guild.md) violations of consent.
- [Amelia](../characters/Amelia_Hawthorne.md) must be treated as a person, never as a machine or specimen.
- No current member knows the full consequence of the Gardens recognizing someone as an intruder.
- The true capability of the Quiet Wardens must remain largely hidden early.
- The Gardens should never feel like passive landscaping under complete human control.
