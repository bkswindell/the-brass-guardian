## Complete Plate Description — Visual Evidence Only

The plate is a vertically oriented, heavily handled archival collage on aged parchment. The sheet has darkened edges, creases, torn corners, tape repairs, stains, and layered paper fragments. The principal object dominates the upper center: a large round oxidized-brass crest with a riveted outer rim.

The crest combines several identifying motifs:

- a compass rose at the top, marked `N`, `E`, `S`, and `W`;
- a central lantern enclosed within a toothed gear;
- a pair of raised wings spreading outward from the gear;
- a long horizontal key across the lower portion;
- symmetrical botanical or scrollwork flourishes;
- a dark recessed background that contrasts with the polished brass relief;
- repeated circular fasteners around the perimeter.

The plate documents five physical manifestations of the same emblem:

1. **Principal brass crest:** a large, round, dimensional metal object shown frontally.
2. **Wax seal impression:** a red-brown wax impression preserving the full compass, wings, lantern, gear, and key design.
3. **Worn expedition patch:** a square, frayed dark-fabric patch embroidered in gold thread. The compass, winged lantern, and key remain visible despite edge wear.
4. **Elias Hawthorne’s sketch:** a pencil-and-ink reconstruction on graph paper, with construction circles and a handwritten design correction.
5. **Brass stamp imprint:** a sepia rubbing or stamped impression showing the complete emblem.

Two contextual photographs show the crest applied to expedition equipment. One is embossed on the cover of a strapped leather field journal. The second is mounted on the face of a dark reinforced instrument case. Both objects show extensive use, scratches, worn leather, rivets, and brass fittings.

The upper-right family-history note is pinned beneath folded paper and sits above the Society of Explorers verification stamp. The lower archival-control strip separates condition, classification, and access fields from the archivist notes. No intentional redactions are visible. The only unreadable handwriting is the small verifier’s signature beneath the **VERIFIED** stamp.

## Non-Visual Canon References and Story Context

The plate visually presents the crest as both a Hawthorne family emblem and an expedition seal. The broader canon should determine whether every date, recovery claim, and reference to Jonathan Hawthorne or Eastreach is final historical canon.

The crest’s key motif visually resembles the key imagery associated with [The Six-Key Sigil](003_The_Six_Key_Sigil.md), but the exact relationship remains unresolved unless established in linked story canon.
