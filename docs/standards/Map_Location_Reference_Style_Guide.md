# Aetherhaven Map-Location Reference Style Guide

## Purpose

The official map-reference summaries are the public-facing canonical descriptions attached to [Aetherhaven](../../locations/Aetherhaven.md)'s numbered and lettered map locations.

They are not complete story-guide profiles.

Their purpose is to:

- welcome readers into the world,
- establish the identity and major points of interest of each place,
- create curiosity and wonder,
- support map navigation,
- and preserve future mysteries without revealing their answers.

Detailed location profiles may contain restricted canon, hidden history, antagonist activity, temporal anomalies, and story-development notes. Those details should not automatically appear in the public map reference.

## Numbered Locations 1–24

### Required Voice

Numbered locations should feel:

- welcoming,
- upbeat,
- adventurous,
- colorful,
- full of wonder,
- and lightly mysterious.

Even industrial, governmental, or working-class locations should feel like meaningful parts of a living city rather than grim exposition.

A warning may be included when appropriate, but it should remain a hint rather than a full disclosure.

### Desired Reader Response

The reader should think:

> I want to visit this place.

or:

> Something curious may happen here.

### Content Requirements

Each summary should generally include:

1. The location's civic or cultural role.
2. Its defining visual or atmospheric character.
3. Its important named points of interest.
4. One intriguing detail, rumor, or gentle mystery.
5. A light hint of caution only where needed.

### Avoid

- Revealing hidden organizations or future betrayals.
- Explaining the true nature of temporal anomalies.
- Confirming rumors that should remain uncertain.
- Turning the entry into a danger report.
- Giving away story resolutions.
- Making ordinary districts sound uniformly threatening.

### Example Tone

> [The Entertainment District](../../locations/The_Entertainment_District.md) glows long after the rest of [Aetherhaven](../../locations/Aetherhaven.md) settles for the evening. Its theaters, music halls, restaurants, mechanical carnivals, and illusion houses draw visitors from every district, while colored aether lamps make its rooftops shimmer across the city. Its most celebrated venue is [the Theatre of Impossible Things](../../locations/The_Theatre_of_Impossible_Things.md), where audiences occasionally recognize a scene they have not lived through yet. The actors insist it is only excellent writing.

## Restricted Locations A–F

### Required Voice

Restricted locations should sound like:

- an official caution,
- a controlled warning,
- a restricted-access notice,
- or an archivist advising the reader not to proceed.

The warning should be credible but alluring.

### Desired Reader Response

The reader should think:

> I know I should not go there—but I desperately want to know what is inside.

### Content Requirements

Each summary should generally include:

1. The visible danger or official reason for restriction.
2. The access status or warning.
3. A striking visual or environmental detail.
4. One unresolved mystery or contradictory report.
5. A closing line that increases curiosity without revealing the truth.

### Avoid

- Fully explaining why the site is dangerous.
- Revealing secret leadership, hidden factions, or late-volume plot twists.
- Making the warning so absolute that exploration feels impossible.
- Describing only danger with no sense of wonder.
- Confirming which rumors are accurate.

### Example Tone

> **Restricted Area — Entry Prohibited.** [The Cauldron](../../locations/The_Cauldron.md) lies in a smoke-filled industrial basin beyond the Ash Line, where furnace towers, salvage markets, slag canals, and crowded workshops operate under laws other than [the High Council](../../organizations/The_High_Council_of_Aetherhaven.md)'s. Ordinary citizens are warned not to cross the Cinder Gates, and [Brass Watch](../../organizations/The_Brass_Watch.md) patrols enter only under exceptional circumstances. [Aetherhaven](../../locations/Aetherhaven.md) officially condemns the district—though many of the city's rarest components still arrive from somewhere beyond those gates.

## Separation of Public and Restricted Canon

Every major location profile should distinguish between:

### Public Map Reference

The spoiler-light text suitable for:

- map legends,
- guidebooks,
- front matter,
- public gazetteers,
- and reader-facing companion pages.

### Full Canonical Profile

The internal story-development record containing:

- politics,
- hidden history,
- antagonist connections,
- temporal evidence,
- staged revelations,
- continuity constraints,
- and unresolved questions.

## Length Guidance

A public map reference should usually be:

- 90–180 words for major numbered locations,
- 75–150 words for smaller numbered locations,
- 90–170 words for restricted locations.

The summary should be rich enough to inspire curiosity but short enough to function beside a map.

## Continuity Rule

Established named points of interest must remain visible in public summaries.

When a location is expanded, do not allow its landmarks, venues, districts, or signature institutions to disappear beneath political or plot detail.
